## 1. Restricted transfers

Sending personal data to a separate organisation outside the UK is a restricted transfer. It is lawful only if the destination is covered by UK adequacy regulations, or the transfer is made under an appropriate safeguard (UK GDPR Article 46), or a specific exception applies. (Background from UK GDPR Chapter V, Articles 44 to 49.)

## 2. The UK-US data bridge

The UK's adequacy regulations for the United States are known as the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, or the UK-US data bridge. They do not cover the United States as a whole. They cover only US organisations that have self-certified to the UK Extension and appear on the Data Privacy Framework List. (ICO, "At a glance"; DSIT factsheet.)

## 3. Who can join

Only US organisations regulated by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) or the US Department of Transportation can take part. Organisations outside those regulators' jurisdiction cannot. The ICO gives examples including banks, insurers, telecommunications providers, certain non-profit organisations not regulated by the FTC, and US government agencies. The FTC's own letter explains that it does not have jurisdiction over most non-profit organisations. (ICO, "What is the UK Extension and the DPF?"; FTC letter.)

## 4. Checks before relying on the data bridge

Before transferring data under the data bridge, a UK organisation should:
1. confirm that the recipient appears on the Data Privacy Framework List;
2. confirm that the recipient has signed up to the UK Extension specifically;
3. check that the recipient's certification covers the type of data being sent (HR or non-HR data). (DSIT factsheet.)

## 5. When the data bridge does not apply

Where there are no adequacy regulations covering the recipient, the transfer needs an Article 46 safeguard. The tools recognised for UK transfers include the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA), the EU standard contractual clauses with the UK Addendum, binding corporate rules, and approved codes of conduct or certification schemes.

A transfer risk assessment should be carried out for any restricted transfer made under an Article 46 tool, and kept under review. The ICO provides a tool for this. (Brodies summary of ICO guidance.)
