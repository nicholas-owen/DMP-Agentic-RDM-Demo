# Where human sequence data can be deposited

**Digest of:**

- EMBL-EBI, ArrayExpress (Annotare) help, "Data access policy": https://ebi-gene-expression-group.github.io/annotare-help/pages/after_sub_data_availability.html

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the sources on 29 October 2026.

## 1. ArrayExpress and the European Nucleotide Archive

ArrayExpress, at EMBL-EBI, archives functional genomics experiments, mostly from non-human samples and from human samples that cannot identify the donor. For sequencing experiments submitted through ArrayExpress, the raw read files are stored in the Sequence Read Archive of the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA). (EMBL-EBI, "Sequencing submissions".)

## 2. Public release

Data in ArrayExpress become public on a set release date or when the associated paper is published. For sequencing experiments, raw read files cannot be accessed until the experiment is public, and there is no infrastructure for privately held files in the Sequence Read Archive. Once public, the data are openly available. (EMBL-EBI, "Data access policy".)

## 3. Human data that could identify donors

Sensitive human data that could identify donors, such as genomic DNA sequences, should not rely on ArrayExpress's pre-release private status, which does not provide the encryption and access control such data need. Such data should be submitted to the European Genome-phenome Archive (EGA), with a data access committee overseeing access. (EMBL-EBI, "Data access policy".)

Identifiable human data can be submitted to ArrayExpress only where participants have consented to public release of the data, normally with the approval of the relevant ethics committee. Making sure this is the case is the submitter's responsibility. (EMBL-EBI, "Sequencing submissions".)

## 4. The European Genome-phenome Archive

The EGA is a service for sharing personally identifiable genetic and phenotypic data. Access to data in the EGA is granted only after a formal application, which is decided by the data access committee for that data set. (SciLifeLab, "Sharing human data".)
