# Legal basis for processing personal data in health research

**Digest of:** Health Research Authority (HRA), GDPR guidance: "Consent in research" and "Legal basis for processing data".

**Sources:**
- https://www.hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/policies-standards-legislation/data-protection-and-information-governance/gdpr-guidance/what-law-says/consent-research/
- https://hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/policies-standards-legislation/data-protection-and-information-governance/gdpr-detailed-guidance/legal-basis-processing-data

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the sources on 29 October 2026.

## 1. Two separate requirements

To process personal data for health and care research lawfully, two things are needed. First, a legal basis under data protection law. Second, compliance with any other legal frameworks that apply, which may include consent to take part in research and the common law duty of confidentiality. The two are separate, and meeting one does not meet the other.

## 2. Legal basis for universities and other public authorities

For universities, NHS organisations, Research Council institutes and other public authorities, the legal basis for processing personal data for research should be that processing is necessary for a task carried out in the public interest (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e)). Public authorities cannot rely on legitimate interests when carrying out their public tasks. They should record why research is a public task, by reference to the purpose set out in statute or their charter.

If the research could be carried out without processing personal data, the public task basis will not be valid.

## 3. Why consent should not be the legal basis

The HRA's position is that the legal basis for processing personal data for health and social care research should not be consent. As a result, the UK GDPR requirements that attach to consent do not apply to health and care research.

The guidance gives reasons:
- Consent is not appropriate as a legal basis where there is an imbalance of power between the controller and the person, for example where the controller is a public authority.
- Consent should not be the legal basis where the rights that follow from it cannot be honoured, for example where withdrawal would undermine the validity of the research.
- If consent is the legal basis and a participant withdraws it, there is no longer any legal basis to process their personal data.

## 4. Special category data

Health data and genetic data are special category data. Processing them needs a further condition as well as a legal basis. For research, the condition is that processing is necessary for archiving, scientific or historical research or statistical purposes, subject to appropriate safeguards, and in the public interest (UK GDPR Article 9(2)(j)).

## 5. Consent to take part still matters

None of this removes the need to seek consent to take part in research, which remains an ethical requirement. Consent is also how the common law duty of confidentiality is usually met, for example where people outside the care team need access to confidential patient information. Relying on public task does not by itself satisfy the duty of confidentiality.

Participants can be asked to consent to take part while being told that their data will be processed as a task in the public interest.
