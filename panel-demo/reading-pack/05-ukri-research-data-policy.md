# UKRI research data policy

**Digest of:** UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), "UKRI research data policy", published 14 October 2026.

**Source:** https://www.ukri.org/publications/uk-research-and-innovation-research-data-policy/ukri-research-data-policy/

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the source on 29 October 2026. Section names in brackets are the policy's own headings.

## 1. What must be shared ("What to share")

Two kinds of research data must be shared with as few restrictions on access and reuse as possible: data needed to understand and validate published findings, and data with long-term value for research and innovation. Shared data must be deposited in an appropriate repository and prepared to FAIR standards.

## 2. Metadata, identifiers and licences ("What to share"; "Licences")

Data must be curated and shared with enough descriptive metadata and supporting documentation for others to understand and reuse them. Data must have a unique and persistent identifier, such as a DOI. Data and metadata must be shared under a clear and accessible licence, which should permit free reuse where possible.

## 3. Timing and exclusive use ("When to share"; "Exclusive use"; "Maximum embargoes on sharing")

UKRI encourages sharing data as early as possible. A limited and defined period of exclusive use by the research team is reasonable, depending on the nature and value of the data, and must be justified in the funding application and data management plan. Data with long-term reuse value that have not yet been published must be shared no later than two years from the end of UKRI funding.

## 4. Restrictions on sharing ("Managing restrictions on sharing"; "Ethics"; Annex 2)

Restrictions for legal, regulatory, security, ethical, contractual or commercial reasons are acceptable when justified, and should be as narrow as possible. Data management and sharing must follow applicable ethical standards. Protecting individuals' privacy is a recognised reason for limiting access. For data about people, the policy points to UKRI's separate guidance on sharing data about people.

## 5. Preservation and retention ("Preserving and retaining data")

Decisions on what to keep should weigh legal requirements, the value of the data, cost and environmental impact. Data that underpin published findings should normally be kept for at least 10 years after publication.

## 6. Data management plans ("Planning research")

Applicants usually outline their approach to data management at application, and some data-intensive funding opportunities require a full plan. Funded projects must have a full data management plan within six months of the start of the award, and keep it under review.

## 7. Costs ("Support for costs")

Funding applications should include the expected costs of managing and sharing research data.

## 8. Data access statements ("Data access statement")

Research publications must include a data access statement saying how the underlying data can be accessed, citing the persistent identifier of the data or its metadata record.
