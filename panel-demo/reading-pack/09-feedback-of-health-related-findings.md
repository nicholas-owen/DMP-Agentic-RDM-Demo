# Telling research participants about health-related findings

**Digest of:** Medical Research Council (MRC), "Telling research participants about health-related findings", which describes the MRC and Wellcome "Framework on the feedback of health-related findings in research" (developed in 2014 with the Health Research Authority, and described by the MRC as current).

**Sources:**
- https://www.ukri.org/who-we-are/mrc/our-policies-and-standards/research/telling-research-participants-about-health-related-findings/
- https://ukri.org/publications/framework-on-the-feedback-of-health-related-findings-in-research/

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the sources on 29 October 2026.

## 1. Why the framework exists

Research can produce findings about an individual participant that have potential implications for their health. The example the MRC gives is a genome-wide association study revealing that a participant has an elevated risk of Alzheimer's disease. The framework helps researchers and research ethics committees identify and consider the issues such findings raise.

## 2. A feedback policy is required

Researchers are required to have a policy on whether health-related findings will be fed back to individual participants. The policy must be clearly set out, and researchers must be able to justify it. The balance of benefit and harm, to individuals and to the public, has to be assessed study by study.

## 3. Participants must be told

Information on the study's feedback policy must be included in the consent process, so participants know before they agree to take part whether and how findings will be fed back.

## 4. If findings will be fed back

Where the policy is to feed back findings to individuals, the researchers must develop a practical feedback pathway that is adequately resourced.
