# Research with adults who lack, or lose, capacity to consent

**Digest of:** Health Research Authority (HRA), "Mental Capacity Act", guidance on the research provisions of the Mental Capacity Act 2005 (England and Wales).

**Source:** https://hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/policies-standards-legislation/mental-capacity-act

Related legislation: Mental Capacity Act 2005, sections 30 to 34: https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/part/1/crossheading/research/enacted

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the sources on 29 October 2026.

## 1. Lawful authority for research

Sections 30 to 33 of the Act provide lawful authority for intrusive research involving adults who lack capacity to consent, provided the research has been approved by an appropriate body. Section 34 deals only with research that began before 1 October 2007.

## 2. The appropriate body

The appropriate body approves the research and checks that the conditions in section 31 are met. In England and Wales, NHS research ethics committees, the Social Care Research Ethics Committee and recognised Ministry of Defence ethics committees can act as appropriate bodies. University ethics committees are not recognised as appropriate bodies under the Act.

## 3. Conditions for approval

The research must be connected with an impairing condition affecting the participant, or its treatment, and it must not be possible to do equally effective research with people who have capacity. Researchers must not continue if the participant appears to object, except to protect them from harm or relieve pain. The participant's interests must always be assumed to outweigh those of science and society.

## 4. Consultees

Researchers must consult someone about the participant's likely wishes. A personal consultee is someone who cares for the person, unpaid, or is interested in their welfare. If there is no one suitable, a nominated consultee who is independent of the project may be used. A consultee gives advice, not consent; the researcher makes the decision.

## 5. Loss of capacity during a study

Consent given while a participant had capacity is generally not valid, under common law, once they lose capacity. To continue intrusive research with that participant, the study needs approval under section 30 and advice from a consultee. If the participant is instead withdrawn, and their data are destroyed or anonymised, neither is needed.

## 6. Regaining capacity

If a participant in a study approved under the Act regains capacity, their consent must be sought to continue. If they do not wish to remain in the study, they must be withdrawn.
