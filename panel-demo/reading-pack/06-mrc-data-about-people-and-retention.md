# MRC: sharing data about people, and retaining research data

**Digest of:**
- Medical Research Council (MRC), "MRC data sharing policy: guidance for sharing data about people": https://www.ukri.org/publications/mrc-guidance-on-sharing-research-data-from-population-and-patient-studies/mrc-data-sharing-policy-guidance-for-sharing-data-about-people
- MRC Regulatory Support Centre, "Retention framework for research data and records", version 2, November 2022: https://www.ukri.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/MRC-100323-RegulatorySupportCentre-RetentionFrameworkResearchDataRecords.pdf

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the sources on 29 October 2026. Section numbers in brackets refer to the MRC guidance.

## 1. Data management plans (guidance 1.1)

Data management plans are the MRC's main way of making sure that funded research produces data that can be shared. Plans must set out the planned data sharing.

## 2. Participant information and consent (guidance 1.3, 1.5)

Participant information sheets and consent forms must be clear about how the research team will make data available. Where confidential information will be processed in order to share it, consent for that processing should be sought. Projects must also comply with data protection law, including providing transparency information that supports the planned sharing.

## 3. Exclusive use (guidance 3.2)

A limited and defined period of exclusive use of the data by the research team is reasonable, and must be set out in the data management plan. The guidance gives six months after the end of the grant as an example.

## 4. Levels of access (guidance 4.3, 4.4)

The guidance describes four levels of access to data about people: unrestricted access, unrestricted controlled access, restricted controlled access (access through a controlled process with constraints on use), and no access. Unrestricted access is suitable only where the risk of disclosure is extremely low, such as summary tables. Projects should follow the Five Safes framework where appropriate, with access governance proportionate to the nature and scale of the project. Teams may run their own access process, use an institutional service, or deposit data in a repository that manages access.

## 5. Access decisions and agreements (guidance 4.5 to 4.8)

Access processes must include accountable, independent input: some of the people involved in decisions must not be directly connected with the data. The MRC strongly encourages public involvement in access decisions. Data sharing must only go ahead under an appropriate data sharing agreement, and sharing activity must be reported to the MRC during the funding period.

## 6. Reasons for not sharing (guidance 4.10)

Where data cannot be shared, the proposal must explain why, or state that data will be de-identified to a standard that allows sharing. Lack of expertise, resources or funding is unlikely to be accepted as a reason not to share.

## 7. Retention periods (retention framework, section 1.1)

The framework sets minimum retention periods:
- basic research: at least 10 years after the study is completed;
- population health and clinical studies: at least 20 years after the study is completed.

Longer periods may be needed in some cases. The periods apply where UKRI is the employer or sponsor; for research the MRC only funds, they are advisory and the sponsor's or institution's own policy applies. Archiving arrangements must be described in funding proposals (guidance section 5).
