# When a data protection impact assessment is required

**Digest of:** Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), "Examples of processing 'likely to result in high risk'", part of the ICO's guidance on data protection impact assessments (DPIAs).

**Source:** https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/examples-of-processing-likely-to-result-in-high-risk

Digest in the preparer's own words, checked against the source on 29 October 2026.

## 1. The general duty

UK GDPR Article 35 requires a controller to carry out a DPIA before starting processing that is likely to result in a high risk to people's rights and freedoms. The ICO publishes a list of types of processing for which it requires a DPIA. The list does not replace the general duty: every proposed processing operation must still be assessed against it. The ICO also regards carrying out a DPIA as good practice even where high risk is not expected.

## 2. Genetic data

The ICO list includes genetic data. Any processing of genetic data requires a DPIA, other than processing by an individual GP or health professional for the purpose of providing health care directly to the person concerned. The ICO's examples of where this applies include medical diagnosis, DNA testing and medical research.

## 3. Other types of processing on the list

The list also covers, among others:
- innovative technology, or a novel use of existing technology, including artificial intelligence;
- biometric data used to identify people;
- matching or combining data sets from different sources;
- invisible processing, where personal data were not obtained from the people they relate to;
- tracking people's behaviour or location;
- targeting children or other vulnerable people;
- processing where a data breach could put people's physical health or safety at risk.
