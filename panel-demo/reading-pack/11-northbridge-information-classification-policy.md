*The University of Northbridge is fictional. This policy was written for a training exercise.*

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how University information is classified and how each class must be handled. It applies to all staff, students, visitors and contractors who handle University information, including research data, wherever the information is held and whatever device is used.

## 2. Classification levels

Every item of University information has one of four classifications. Where information combines elements of different levels, the highest level applies to the whole.

| Level | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved for release to anyone. | Published papers; open data sets; the prospectus. |
| Internal | For University use; disclosure would cause little harm. | Internal procedures; committee papers not marked otherwise. |
| Confidential | Disclosure could harm individuals or the University. | Personal data that is not special category; unpublished research results not containing personal data; gene-level summary tables, such as expression counts per sample, that contain no sequence or genotype information, once released from the NSRE through output checking. |
| Highly Confidential | Disclosure could cause serious harm to individuals or the University. | Special category personal data, including health and genetic data, about identifiable or pseudonymised individuals; human genetic sequence data from research participants, including raw reads, alignments and variant calls; clinical or health records linked to research participants; pseudonymisation keys. |

## 3. Classifying research data

Research teams must record the classification of their data in the project's data management plan. Data derived from Highly Confidential data keep that classification unless they have been aggregated or anonymised and approved for release through output checking (see the NSRE user guide). Pseudonymised data keep the classification of the data they were derived from.

## 4. Handling rules

| Rule | Public | Internal | Confidential | Highly Confidential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Where it may be stored and processed | Anywhere | University systems | University-managed storage, including group shared drives | The Northbridge Secure Research Environment (NSRE) only, or an approved NHS clinical system |
| Laptops and desktop workstations | Permitted | Permitted | University-managed, encrypted devices only | Not permitted, including working or temporary copies |
| Email | Permitted | University email | University email to named recipients | Not permitted |
| Removable media | Permitted | Encrypted only | Encrypted only, with manager approval | Not permitted |
| Sharing outside the University | Permitted | With manager approval | Under a written agreement | Only through the NSRE data release process, under a data sharing agreement approved by the Information Governance Office |
| Transfer outside the UK | Permitted | With manager approval | Information Governance Office approval | As for sharing, plus Information Governance Office approval of the legal transfer mechanism |
| Printing | Permitted | Permitted | Collect immediately | Not permitted |

## 5. Pseudonymisation

Where research uses pseudonymised data, the key linking codes to identities must be held separately from the research data, by the data provider (for example the NHS clinical team) or by a designated custodian. Research teams should not hold keys. Pseudonymised data are still personal data and must be handled at their classification level.

## 6. Data protection impact assessment

Projects that will process Highly Confidential personal data must complete the University's DPIA screening with the Data Protection Officer before processing begins. Where screening identifies a likely high risk, including any processing of genetic data, a full DPIA must be completed and approved before processing begins.

## 7. Exceptions

Any exception to this policy must be approved in writing, in advance, by the Information Governance Office, and recorded. Exceptions are not granted for holding Highly Confidential data outside approved environments for convenience, including for interactive analysis.

## 8. Breaches

Suspected breaches of this policy, including Highly Confidential data found outside approved environments, must be reported to the Information Governance Office within 24 hours.
