# What counts as personal data, and pseudonymised data

## 1. The legal definition

Personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable living individual. A person is identifiable if they can be identified directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number or location data, or by one or more factors specific to their physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural or social identity. (Data Protection Act 2018, section 3.)

## 2. Pseudonymised data is still personal data

The ICO states that information which has had identifiers removed or replaced in order to pseudonymise it is still personal data. Pseudonymisation reduces the risk to individuals, which makes it a useful safeguard, but it does not take the data outside data protection law. (ICO, "What is personal information: a guide", sections "At a glance" and "What is personal data?".)

## 3. Judging identifiability

When deciding whether someone is identifiable, account should be taken of all the means reasonably likely to be used, by the organisation holding the data or by any other person, to identify them. The ICO suggests considering what an interested and sufficiently determined person could do. The likelihood of identification can change over time, for example as other data become available, so the assessment has to be kept under review. (ICO, "What is personal information: a guide", sections "At a glance" and "Can we identify an individual indirectly?".)

## 4. Genomic sequence data

EMBL-EBI's ArrayExpress guidance treats human genomic DNA sequences as sensitive data that could identify the people they come from, and directs such data to a controlled access archive rather than to public release. (EMBL-EBI, "Data access policy", section on human identifiable data. See also document 07.)

## 5. Status of the ICO guidance

The ICO notes that its guidance on personal information is under review following changes made by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, and may change.
